# Campaign audit, 2026-08-21 — all 5 campaigns, post-bugfix

**Run:** `buildBattery` at content `c08ceb4`, 150 builds × 25 games per cell,
156 cells (5 campaigns × all encounters × 4 difficulties) ≈ **585,000 games.**
Raw per-build data: `balance_runs/audit_<campaign>_c08ceb4.json`.

Judged against `DIFFICULTY_TARGETS.md` (owner-ratified 2026-08-21). Re-report
any campaign against the *current* thresholds without re-simming:

```bash
npx tsx src/ai/buildBattery.ts --merge balance_runs/audit_sealeddeep_c08ceb4.json
```

⚠ **This audit supersedes every previous campaign balance verdict.** All prior
verdicts were produced either by the buggy sampler (duplicate-class parties +
unearned boons) or by the 3-representative-party mean, which is a biased-low
estimator. See `REBALANCE_2026-08.md` for the bug history.

## Headline

| campaign | cells | OK | TOO HARD | TOO EASY | WALLS | shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| goblinopolis | 20 | 13 | 0 | 7 | 0 | soft late |
| lantern | 20 | 12 | 0 | 6 | 2 | soft late |
| moonberry | 20 | 11 | 0 | 8 | 1 | soft late |
| **sealeddeep** | 48 | 17 | **21** | 8 | **22** | **brutal first half, soft second** |
| unlitbeacon | 48 | 20 | 3 | 23 | 5 | soft throughout, one brutal cell |

**Two distinct problems, not one.** Sealed Deep is over-tuned; everything else
is under-tuned. They need opposite fixes and must not be batched together.

## Finding 1 — Sealed Deep has an INVERTED difficulty curve

Its first half is the hardest content in the game and its second half is among
the softest.

| encounters | levels | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| e1–e6 | L1–L5 | almost universally TOO HARD + WALLS |
| e7–e12 | L6–L10 | mostly TOO EASY or OK |

Worst cells (floor = share of teams that must reach the difficulty's target):

| cell | difficulty | teams solving | needed | walled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| e1 | **easy** | 8.7% | 65% | **39%** |
| e1 | nightmare | **0.0%** | 20% | **94%** |
| e2 | easy | 14.7% | 65% | 42% |
| e4 | nightmare | **0.0%** | 10% | **99%** |
| e6 | hard | 3.3% | 20% | 81% |

Read the first row plainly: **on EASY, on the opening fight, 39% of parties
cannot progress at all** and fewer than one in ten plays it at the level "easy"
is supposed to feel like. e4/nightmare walls 99% of parties — not one sampled
team in 150 could clear 40%.

### Why — and it maps 1:1 onto the boon bug

Sealed Deep is the only campaign tuned on `buildBattery` while it was buggy. The
boon bug gave **every** cell the campaign's full 2-boon set, but boons are only
earnable from e7 onward. So:

- **e1–e6** were measured with 2 unearned boons → looked far easier than they
  play → the tuner raised enemy HP to compensate → genuinely brutal.
- **e7–e12** legitimately carry boons → were measured roughly right → landed
  near-correct, and now read soft only because of the estimator change.

The break in the audit falls exactly at e6/e7, which is exactly where boons
begin. The fingerprint is confirmed by comparing scale tables for the same slots
against Unlit Beacon (tuned on the clean harness):

| encounter | Sealed Deep easy / nightmare | Unlit Beacon easy / nightmare |
|---|---|---|
| e1 | 1.40 / 1.95 | 0.95 / 1.32 |
| e2 | 1.10 / 1.42 | 0.80 / 0.98 |
| e6 | 1.30 / 2.00 | 0.70 / 1.55 |

Sealed Deep's opening fights carry ~45–50% more enemy HP than the equivalent
fights in the campaign that was never measured through the bug.

**Direction: e1–e6 need substantial scale REDUCTIONS.** Expect to land near
Unlit Beacon's values for the same slots. e7–e12 need mild raises at easy/medium
only.

## Finding 2 — everything else is too soft, and only in LATE encounters

Across the trilogy and Unlit Beacon: **0 too-hard cells anywhere past L2**, and
44 too-easy cells, overwhelmingly late.

| campaign | early (L≤2) | later (L≥3) |
|---|---|---|
| goblinopolis | 0 easy | 7 easy |
| lantern | 2 easy | 4 easy |
| moonberry | 0 easy | 8 easy |
| unlitbeacon | 4 easy / 3 hard | **19 easy** / 0 hard |

Worst ceiling breaches (median team's win rate vs what the tier permits):

| cell | difficulty | median | ceiling | over by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| unlitbeacon e4 | nightmare | 70% | 45% | **+25** |
| unlitbeacon e4 | hard | 88% | 65% | +23 |
| unlitbeacon e12 | hard | 88% | 65% | +23 |
| unlitbeacon e6 | nightmare | 64% | 45% | +19 |
| goblinopolis e4 | medium | 96% | 80% | +16 |

A nightmare cell where the *median* team wins 70% of its games is not nightmare;
it is easier than several campaigns' "hard". Unlit Beacon e4 and e6 are the
clearest offenders.

**Mechanism:** party power compounds with level (specials L2–L3, passives
L4–L5, Deep Gifts L7–L8) while enemy scaling stays roughly flat, so campaigns
get *easier* as they progress. That is backwards, and it is a scaling-CURVE
problem, not a set of independent per-cell problems. Tuning the cells one at a
time will work but will be slow and will not stop it recurring in campaign 6.

## Finding 3 — difficulty tiers are compressed, especially late

Too-easy failures spread evenly across all four tiers (16 easy / 16 medium /
12 hard / 8 nightmare), so no single tier is miscalibrated — the whole ladder
is squashed toward the top. Sharpest examples:

- **goblinopolis e4**: easy/medium/hard ALL breach (hard median 80%), then
  nightmare is fine at 28%. Three tiers are nearly indistinguishable, then a
  cliff.
- **unlitbeacon e4**: all four tiers breach, nightmare median 70%.

Where three tiers of a single encounter are within a few points of each other,
the fix is the *spread* of that encounter's scale row, not each cell.

## Finding 4 — walls concentrate in two places

22 of the 30 WALLS flags are Sealed Deep (a symptom of Finding 1). The other
notable cluster is **unlitbeacon e11** — the boss duel — walling 15% / 25% /
33% of teams on easy / medium / hard. That encounter has a documented breakpoint
cliff (`CAMPAIGN3_BALANCE_NOTES.md`: ranged party 3% at scale 0.88, 100% at
0.89, as wisp HP 29→30 crosses a one-shot threshold). Treat it as a structural
fix, not a scale nudge — walking the scale will teleport rather than slide.

## Finding 5 — Unlit Beacon e3 is the one early cell that is too hard

`e3` (L2, the "hold") reads TOO HARD at hard and nightmare, with 61% of teams
walled on nightmare and a median of 4%. It is an EARLY encounter, where the
owner explicitly wants forgiveness. Note its history: the guards were moved
ONTO the marks during campaign-3 balancing to make the cell scale-responsive
(`CAMPAIGN3_BALANCE_NOTES.md` §3.2). That change worked and then some.

## Method notes worth keeping

- **150 builds/cell is enough at every level.** Verified by two disjoint
  150-build samples of the same cell: solve-share differed 0.0 pts at e1 (214%
  coverage) and 3.3 pts at e12 (0.0001% coverage), the latter inside binomial
  noise. Coverage collapses with level; precision does not. Full data in
  `DIFFICULTY_TARGETS.md`.
- **Never gate on a max.** The same test showed `best build` swinging 96% vs
  84% across the two samples. The retired acceptance check gated nightmare on
  exactly that statistic.
- **`⚠ bimodal`** in the report (mean/median gap > 15 pts) means the cell is not
  "medium difficulty" — it is a coin flip on comp. sealeddeep e5/hard and
  e7/nightmare, unlitbeacon e11 are the live examples. Tune the brick, not the
  average.

## Suggested order of work

1. **Sealed Deep e1–e6 scale reductions.** Biggest, most player-visible harm —
   a 39%-walled opening fight on EASY kills runs before the campaign starts.
2. **Unlit Beacon e3** (too hard, early) and **e11** (walls, structural).
3. **The soft-late problem** — decide curve vs per-cell before touching content.
   Worth a design call: if enemy scaling should track party power growth, that
   is one change to the ladder rather than ~44 cell edits.
4. **Trilogy** — owner has said re-tuning is not urgent (free content that
   plays acceptably). Bring findings before changing shipped content.

⚠ Do not batch 1 and 3 into one pass. They pull in opposite directions and a
combined pass will be impossible to attribute when re-measured.
